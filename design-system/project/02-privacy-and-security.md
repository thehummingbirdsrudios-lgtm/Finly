# Privacy and security patterns

Safety controls are never removed to save a tap; they are designed to feel light: one inline step, at the moment it is needed.

## Who can see this

- Show a **PrivacyBadge** on anything not visible to everyone in the business: Private, Family, Business, Restricted, Shared — or the owner's own level names. The reason line explains the rule, never the content.
- **Personal finance is private to its owner by default.** No other person — family, worker, Admin or Super Admin — sees it unless the owner grants it, and every grant is audited. Administering the system is not the same as seeing money.
- **Hidden things do not exist on screen.** They never appear in search, suggestions, recent searches, pickers, filters, counts, totals, charts, notifications, exports or "no results" wording. When a user meets something they may know of but not open, use **AccessState** with a short reason and a way forward.
- Navigation, buttons and settings follow the user's actual permissions, not the name of their role. A worker sees Add entry, My entries, Assigned, Pending and Notifications — and after saving a blind entry, only "Entry recorded."
- The phone's view is only convenience. The server decides every permission and every number.

## Unlock and step-up

- **Three different checks, three different moments:** signing in (who are you), unlocking this phone (fingerprint or M-PIN), and step-up before a sensitive action. M-PIN unlocks this phone only and is never the password.
- Returning users see **UnlockScreen**: fingerprint first, then M-PIN, then password.
- High-risk actions — large transfers, ownership or visibility changes, confidential shares, permission changes — open one **StepUpSheet** that names exactly what will happen and why the check is needed. If it fails, nothing happens and the sheet says so.
- Session expired mid-form: a re-authentication sheet over the form; the draft is kept encrypted and the user resumes where they were.

## Sharing proof

The only proof surfaces are generated ones: a message, a Photo Proof card, a PDF, a secure PDF and the Secure Viewer — all built from the posted record. **Never ask for, suggest or accept a screenshot as proof.**

Every external share follows the same short path, each step one screen or sheet:

1. Choose the format (the user's default is preselected).
2. **Exact preview** — MessagePreview text, the ProofCard image, or the PdfPage — exactly what will be sent.
3. Security — **SecurityBuilder** with the policy applied: mandatory controls locked on, blocked ones locked off, the stricter rule winning.
4. Recipient check and **ShareConfirm**: recipient, contact, channel, format, security, content, then **Verify & share** (with step-up when the policy asks).

Rules:

- If the content, recipient, permission or security setting changes after the preview, the verification is void: ShareConfirm says so and the button stays disabled until the user reviews again.
- Delivery status is honest. A hand-off to WhatsApp shows "Handed to WhatsApp"; "Sent" appears only when the channel confirms it. Cancelling or a failed hand-off records nothing as sent.
- Never claim a downloaded or forwarded file can be recalled. For highly confidential proof, suggest Secure Viewer, which can expire and be revoked ("This secure document has expired." / "This document is no longer available.").
- Proof cards and PDFs carry a recipient-specific watermark when the policy asks, a document or verification code, and the statement "Generated by Finly from the posted record, not a screenshot."

## Screen privacy

- Screens are classified. Confidential and owner-only screens block screen capture and blank their recent-apps preview; ordinary screens do not, so the whole app is not punished for its most sensitive page.
- Lock-screen notifications follow the user's setting — full, masked or generic ("A financial update requires your attention.") — and never show a restricted amount. Opening one requires unlocking first, then goes straight to the right screen.
- After the configured time in the background the app locks; highly confidential policy locks immediately.
